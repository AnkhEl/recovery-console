# Coordination simulator v4: movement and mobile sightings

`coordination-simulator-v4` uses only synthetic incident data. It makes two separately reviewed changes: continuous movement within each one-second simulation tick, then mobile-unit sightings. The v2 posted-junction observation conditions and v3 footprint check remain in force. This is a simulator change, not a model experiment. The saved `observation-ranker-v3` weights and reports are historical; AI-generated advice remains disabled.

## Movement correction and paired v3 comparison

In v3 an actor stopped at the end of its first directed edge for the rest of a tick, even when time and speed remained. In v4, each actor spends its remaining tick time on legal connected edges. The vehicle selects a new branch when it reaches each node, using unit positions at that same within-tick instant. Current synthetic traffic is constant inside a tick; the usual 15-second updates still apply. Unit objectives, order timing, seeded branch policy, five-second device reports, and deterministic action replay remain. An actor may now reach an objective, traverse several short edges, or encounter another actor inside one tick. Arrival activity is shown at the first integer display second after the exact arrival.

The frozen [v3 baseline](movement-v3-baseline.json) was captured **before** editing the integrator. The [v4 result](movement-v4-results.json) was captured afterward with the same graph, actions, visibility data, and seeds. `scripts/compare-movement.mjs` records a diagnostic branch trace in an offline bundle only; no hidden branch trace is exposed in the app. The paired set has seeds 1–16, 27, and 42, each with no order, Unit 02 posted at Sandown, Unit 03 posted at Norwich, or Unit 02 posted at Sandown and retasked to Grayston at 01:00: 72 ordinary three-minute runs. These were selected to inspect movement effects, not held out for policy or model evaluation.

| Measure                               | Paired v3 → v4 movement result                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Vehicle branch sequences              | Changed in 72/72 runs; 4,534 → 5,230 branch decisions                                                                                                                                                        |
| Confirmed vehicle position series     | Changed in 72/72 runs                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Unit arrival activity                 | Changed in 36/72 runs. Unit 03 to Norwich arrived 3–9 seconds earlier (median six) in all 18 such runs; Unit 02's initial Sandown arrival remained 00:01. Retask arrival also changed in all 18 retask runs. |
| Posted-junction observation sequences | Changed in 8/72 runs; eight v3 events versus five v4 events in the paired set                                                                                                                                |

For example, seed 2 with Unit 03 posted at Norwich changed from one observation at 00:47 (available 00:49) to observations at 00:42 and 02:01 (available 00:44 and 02:03). Seed 9 with Unit 02 posted at Sandown gained an observation at 02:58 (available 03:00). Seed 27 with that same post lost its two v3 observations at 01:01 and 02:45, while seed 42 with Unit 03 at Norwich lost its v3 00:51 event. These changes are expected consequences of different within-tick vehicle branches and timings; they are **not** mobile-sighting gains or losses. The old seed 27 browser fixture must therefore remain historical. The v4 posted GPS-loss example uses seed 413, with observation at 01:02 and availability at 01:04; the marker stays at the 00:55 device report.

## Separate mobile-sighting rules

Mobile sightings use movement inside the tick, the directed road graph, and synthetic two-dimensional sight lines. They do **not** count as posted-junction observations, change the ranker's historical target, or become confirmed vehicle device reports. The unit must be moving on a directed road edge, and both unit and vehicle speeds must be at least **5 km/h**. Samples cover each tick in 0.05-second intervals and check the start, middle, and end of each interval; a duration accumulates only across continuously valid intervals. The road-corridor relation can cross connected short edges when they share a street name and each turn differs by at most **25°** from the corridor heading. A different road name or a nonlegal connection breaks it.

| Encounter     | Synthetic position, heading, range, and duration assumptions                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Oncoming pass | The vehicle is ahead of the unit on an opposite directed edge of the same legal corridor; headings differ by at least **155°**; forward corridor separation is at most **35 m** for **1.5 continuous seconds**. A pass already separating does not qualify. |
| Following     | The unit is behind the vehicle on the same directed corridor; headings differ by at most **25°**; vehicle is **8–45 m** ahead for **2 continuous seconds**. A vehicle behind the unit does not qualify.                                                     |

Each checked interval must also pass the existing style of seeded synthetic visibility check (one in five hashed visibility windows is obscured) and have no intersection with a mapped footprint. The footprint extract is incomplete, so a clear mapped ray is labelled **actual visibility uncertain**, never verified real-world visibility. This two-dimensional rule has no building heights, terrain, windows, vehicle occlusion, lane separation, or human gaze model. The event becomes available **two seconds after the observed second**. It names the reporting unit, encounter type, and approximate road corridor. It includes no vehicle coordinates, device report, or hidden branch score. During a GPS outage it may be reported after the delay while the last confirmed vehicle marker and report time remain fixed. Unit patrols can produce mobile sightings without an operator order; an order can change whether the paths meet.

In the 72 paired ordinary v4 runs, the new rule produced **four** mobile events in four runs, all following, and no oncoming events. Those counts are separate from the v3/v4 movement comparison. They indicate only what happened in this selected ordinary set; they are not an interception rate or model score. Controlled fixtures exercise both encounter types and negatives.

## Reproducible browser fixtures

These are labelled development-only fixtures on the real directed graph with synthetic starts and a synthetic vehicle GPS outage from 00:01 until 00:20 (the signal resumes at 00:20). Use `?scenarioSeed=4&mobileFixture=…`; at 00:00 assign Unit 02 to **5th Street × Park Lane**. The options below are alternatives, not a combined run.

| Fixture value         | Scrub to | Expected operator view                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------- | -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `oncoming-clear`      | 00:06    | Unit 02 mobile oncoming sighting observed 00:04, available 00:06; map and activity identify Park Lane, while the vehicle GPS marker remains at 00:00. |
| `following-clear`     | 00:04    | Unit 02 mobile following sighting observed 00:02, available 00:04; same fixed confirmed vehicle marker.                                               |
| `oncoming-fleeting`   | 00:19    | No mobile sighting: the pass is too brief.                                                                                                            |
| `oncoming-separating` | 00:19    | No mobile sighting: the actors are already moving apart.                                                                                              |
| `following-behind`    | 00:19    | No mobile sighting: the vehicle is behind the unit.                                                                                                   |

The `oncoming-clear` and `following-clear` observations are absent before availability. Rewind to confirm the same result, and reset to clear the order. The early outage is fixture state rather than a future operator action, so the normal assignment confirmation flow remains intact. `tests/mobile-sightings.test.mjs` also covers mapped obstruction suppression, connected short-edge corridor continuity, replay determinism, and hidden-position separation. `scripts/mobile-sighting-browser-review.mjs` checks the clear and fleeting UI cases and marker integrity. None of these fixtures is evidence that a real moving officer would see a real vehicle.

No new key, backend, hosted model, service, or recurring inference cost is introduced. The map and frozen OSM footprints retain their existing attribution and licence terms.
